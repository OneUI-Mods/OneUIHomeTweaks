# Glass appearance

The Appearance tab has individual opt-in switches for Quick Panel tiles, the notification panel and cards, the volume-key panel, and the Home dock and folders. Glass intensity runs from 0 to 100%. The separate tint slider runs from 0 to 100% in 5-point increments and defaults to 50%. Live hooks use the SM-S948Q One UI 9 BZI9 contract (Android 17). Other builds can use the preview. The feature uses the existing first-unlock gate, crash protection and per-host switches.

## iOS 27 reference

The iPhone 16 Pro restore image for iOS 27.2.1 (`24B156`) was read from [Apple's restore CDN](https://updates.cdn-apple.com/2026FallFCS/a75ca3b6-83d4-440d-99fc-99ecb7406750/iPhone17,1_27.2.1_24B156_Restore.ipsw). The `BuildManifest.plist` links the SystemOS cryptex used to inspect the ARM64e shared cache.

- QuartzCore's `GlassBackgroundFilter::render` binds the backdrop surface supplied by the renderer and prepares the separate edge blur and color transforms. This call exposed no independent update frequency. The filter runs on Core Animation's rendering path, whose available frame rates depend on the display and system policy. [Apple's ProMotion documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/optimizing-iphone-and-ipad-apps-to-support-promotion-displays) In the AIR for `glass_background_sdf_r_lpf`, each refraction sample computes `a × (1 − √(t × (2 − t)))`, with `t = clamp(depth / height, 0, 1)`. The result moves the texture coordinate along the contour normal. The outer and inner paths use different height and displacement uniforms, two texture samples, and a distance-based blend. Actual values vary by the iOS material recipe.
- CoverSheet's SDF edge backdrop exposes refraction and aberration inputs. `updateBackgroundGlassEffectForDraggingProgress:scaling:usingGlassEffects:` sends `4 × clamp(p, 0, 0.25)` to the background glass's effect multiplier on the unscaled path. The scaled path uses `4 × clamp(p, 0, 0.60) / 0.60`. UserNotificationsUIKit separately tracks the glass state, apparent depth and scroll velocity of list cells. [iOS 27 notification guide](https://support.apple.com/en-il/guide/iphone/iph6534c01bc/27/ios/27)
- CoverSheet and UserNotificationsUIKit pass the interface style, glass luminance and wallpaper luminance to the notification list's glass appearance update. The Android implementation uses One UI's active theme for the material palette and the sampled backdrop luminance for the shader tint.
- ControlCenterUIKit has separate recipes for the dark, module, fill and tertiary materials. ControlCenterUI's module container chooses between standard, subdued and gallery glass and passes a grouping flag. [iOS 27 Control Center guide](https://support.apple.com/en-il/guide/iphone/iph59095ec58/27/ios/27)
- SpringBoard's hardware-volume HUD uses `SBElasticVolumeViewController` and `SBElasticSliderView`. The slider has distinct base and capture-only material views and computes its corner radius from its minor-axis fraction. One UI keeps its own slider geometry and foreground and applies the shared refraction to its background. [Apple's Liquid Glass materials guide](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)
- SpringBoardHome calculates and pixel-aligns the dock plate and icon list separately. For the 402-point portrait profile with standard icons, the default plate uses 17-point side insets and an 80-point icon. Its visible height is 132 points, with 26 points above and below the icon from the 35-point grid inset minus the 17-point plate inset plus the 8-point icon inset. In the glass path, `SBDockView._applyGlass` requests a dock-specific material with grouping enabled and passes a style derived from the current trait collection.
- UIKitCore's tint preference rounds the normalized value `t` to `round(20 × t) / 20` on read and write. The getter falls back to `0.50` when the preference is absent. [Apple's iPhone guide](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/iphone/iphd6804774e/27/ios/27) describes the slider as a range from clearer to more tinted glass.
- SpringBoardHome passes the folder animator's progress `p` to the background handoff. The folder icon's effect and overlay use `1 − p`, and the open folder background uses `p`. Corner radius is interpolated from the closed and open geometry, with a conversion between the visible bounds and the scaled drawing coordinates.
- Apple's [WWDC26 session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/102/?id=206) describes stronger diffusion, a dark edge, brighter specular highlights and the option to tint the glass. Those visual features guided the Android design.

The Android code uses an independently written Canvas painter and lens shaders for AGSL and OpenGL ES. No Apple binaries, shaders or UI assets are bundled.

## One UI implementation

The Quick Panel styles the native containers for long cards, grouped tiles, and the brightness and volume capsules. The cards have a clearer highlight. Groups and slider containers have softer edge lighting so the tiles and the slider fill retain visual priority. Each uses the current rounded outline from the native Compose shape. The tint is behind the content and the reflection follows a single thin edge. Each surface responds to nearby touch and fades the reflection over 210 ms after release. Individual labels and icons are left on Samsung’s drawing path. Long cards lower their stock background opacity so the shade backdrop is visible.

The volume-key panel uses Samsung's compact track background and the independent rim layer of the dual and expanded panels. DexKit resolves Sound Assistant's dialog classes against the installed APK. Sound Assistant's optional effects also have a separate drawing path, where refraction is drawn after the background effect and before the slider contents. The panel shares the live refraction path with the shade, and window transforms align the sampled scene during the animation. The volume panel's theme setting and Samsung's Reduce transparency setting are respected. The background capture stops when the panel leaves the screen. The Android 17 emulator verified the independent preview switch and both themes. Version 0.21.27 drew and sampled the backdrop through the Sound Assistant effects path on the SM-S948Q. The stock and expanded layouts were inspected in the installed APKs but their glass rendering was not verified on the device.

The notification panel uses a restrained neutral tint behind the content. The top edge fades in over the first quarter of Samsung's pull progress. Horizontal transitions update on each change of the native drag fraction, including cancellation, and use the native split-panel state to select the direction. Notification cards inherit the native drawable's bounds, eight corner radii, clipping and fade, including while the card expands. The underlying native blur stays active. The card rim responds to nearby touch and fades over 210 ms after release. The same Notifications switch also styles detailed heads-up banners and Samsung's brief pop-ups. Their glass follows the stock appearance and dismissal motions. Lock-screen notifications keep their stock material.

The Home dock sizes the glass from the available dock width and uses the native icon size to scale the reference padding. Its vertical placement follows the existing icon row within the navigation insets. Samsung continues to position and handle taps for the icons. The wallpaper-only surface is mirrored into a private display limited to the dock's sampling area. It runs at up to 120 Hz. A GPU texture stream renders each new compositor frame directly into the dock's surface below the icons. The center transmits the wallpaper already visible on screen. Refraction blends outward into the inner bevel and outer edge, with sampling coordinates aligned to the dock surface during drawing. The glass stays visible while the shade or Recents temporarily takes focus. The display and textures are released once Home stops or becomes hidden, the screen turns off, or the dock detaches.

If the wallpaper stream cannot start, the dock can still use a half-resolution wallpaper crop. That path schedules captures on display frames and shares the notification panel's idle delay. Samsung’s compositor blur supplies the fallback when the wallpaper is unavailable.

On a return from Recents, Home draws the same transparent tint and rim while the wallpaper stream starts. Only the edge refraction fades in after the first source frame arrives. A fixed sampling band at the bottom of the display keeps the stream attached as the dock moves and scales during the return.

Version 0.21.32 rendered 120 new wallpaper frames in one second of a Galaxy S26 Ultra home paging trace. During partial notification and Recents gestures, the same dock surface stayed attached. In a stationary comparison, the center of the glass matched the underlying wallpaper position within about one vertical pixel after accounting for the movement in the surrounding wallpaper.

Closed folders on regular Home pages, DIY Home pages and the dock use the launcher’s original mini-icon layer over a translucent plate. Existing folder images can request the stock mini-icon preview once, at one folder per frame. Locked folders and closed app drawer folders keep their stock rendering. The temporary folder backdrop receives the material during the native opening and closing animation. Full folders apply it to the bound tray background and popup folders apply it under the folder content. Popup folders use Samsung’s existing folder blur crop for the AGSL lens when the crop is available. The lens has separate inner and outer bending, mild edge color separation and luminance-dependent tint. The bitmap comes from the stock renderer, is used only while the matching folder blur is attached, and is never saved. Turning a switch off withdraws the effect. Reopening Quick Panel lets the native composition redraw its tiles.

The folder glass follows the native One UI animation progress. Opening and closing share the same direction-independent progress, so the temporary glass layer and the open folder backdrop can crossfade using complementary opacity. The radius is calculated in screen pixels and divided by the current view scale for drawing. This keeps the corners continuous as the animation scales the view.

Closed folders use the launcher’s own silhouette for the glass body, rim and mini-icon clipping. Standard preview bitmaps keep their stock coordinates for the opening animation. Preview bitmaps of a different size are fitted and centered with a small margin. The launcher draws badges and labels separately.

The tint setting shares UIKit's steps and default. For the One UI material, normalized tint `q` scales each profile's body tint by `0.35 + 1.3 × q`. At 50% the multiplier is 1. The AGSL preview samples its own artwork and uses the same dock lens settings as the Home hook.

The notification cards and Quick Panel share a reduced-size backdrop under the unlocked shade. Detailed and brief pop-ups get their own temporary backdrop. WindowManager supplies the image below the corresponding window under Samsung's standard screenshot policy. A worker creates a softer copy for the body and a less blurred copy for the bevel. The Android shader uses the circular displacement curve for both paths, with separate One UI heights, offsets and tint. It clips the result to the native corners. The notification lens draws between the stock background and notification content. The image fades in, follows the surface's position on screen and is released when the panel or banner closes or the device locks. Images stay in memory. Samsung's live blur remains the base backdrop and supplies the fallback when no source is available.

While the shade is open and the backdrop is changing, the next capture starts on the next available Android frame callback after the previous capture finishes. There is at most one capture in flight. The display refresh rate defines the ceiling, and Samsung's capture and processing latency determine the delivered rate. Identical captures do not rebuild textures or redraw the cards. When several are identical, the module spaces out sampling by a growing number of display frame intervals until motion resumes. A denied capture discards any previously sampled texture and leaves Samsung's live blur visible. Closing or detaching the shade clears its textures and stops sampling. Pop-up notifications keep their existing short-lived backdrop. [Android Choreographer](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/view/Choreographer)

The live glass surfaces redraw when Android changes between light and dark mode. Light glass uses a brighter, cooler body tint. Dark glass uses a subdued tint with the brighter edge reflection preserved. The preview follows the settings app's theme, which defaults to the system theme. The 0.21.21 preview was checked with the same artwork and tint while switching light → dark → light on Android 17. The tile, notification and dock samples all changed and returned to their original colors.

The BZI9 classes and method contracts were checked against a saved SystemUI, launcher and framework from the Galaxy. The 0.21.11 build was checked during the native folder handoff and at 0% and 100% tint in both the preview and the expanded full folder. The optional Home Up popup layout remains a separate device check.

On SM-S948Q, the 0.21.13 SystemUI run covered vertical panel opening, horizontal handoff, returning from a partial swipe, notification scrolling, and the card, group and slider draw hooks. Tint at 0%, 100% and restored 50% was checked in saved settings. The 0% and 100% captures differed in both panels with the same wallpaper. The iOS references were the 27.2.1 IPSW and the Control Center image in Apple's guide.

The 0.21.16 build displayed the 240 × 520 source through the notification card and all three Quick Panel surface types on the SM-S948Q. Notification card interiors were compared with the feature on and off using the same wallpaper and visible notifications.

The 0.21.18 release preview was compared with 0.21.17 on the Android 17 emulator with all three appearance switches on. The Android shader compiled and rendered there. On the SM-S948Q, 0.21.20 rendered both detailed and brief pop-ups over a striped test screen. The detailed banner sampled below the shade (window type 2040). The brief banner sampled below Samsung's screen-on EdgeLightingWindow (type 2227) in the separate `com.android.systemui:edgelighting` process. Both captures used the stock screenshot policy and reached the lens draw. The detailed banner worked on its first display after a SystemUI reload without opening the notification panel first.

The settings screen separates a process that has not loaded the current module, a hook waiting for its first draw, and a surface for which the module has recorded a draw.
